# sciforce

## Science Bros

Broforce-style run-and-gun platformer: an 18-mission campaign against the Anti-Science League that gets harder mission by mission, three bonus modes (Lab Siege, Boss Rush, Moon Base), 19 trophies, 38 scientist bros (3 of them melee bros), 7 special events, 4 pickup guns, 17 quack types, 20 bosses, a drivable mech, objectives, intel, a world map and in-level maps. Single self-contained HTML file, no build step.

- Game: `science-bros/index.html`. Open it in a browser.
- Controls: arrows or A/D move; Up, W or Space jump (hold against a wall to climb, jump off it to wall-jump); Z or J fire; X or K special (3 per bro); C or L punch; Down + punch in the air for a ground pound; tap left or right twice quickly to run (or push a gamepad stick all the way); Shift or V dodge-roll (also jumps out of the mech); Up/Down on a ladder climbs; hold B or U to aim (you keep facing the same way, so you can walk backwards and keep shooting forwards; Up or W then tilts the gun up: 45 degrees while you move, straight up while you stand; Space still jumps); hold Tab for the map; P pause menu; M mute.
- Gamepad (standard mapping; a PS5 DualSense works over USB-C or Bluetooth): stick or d-pad move (stick all the way runs), Cross/A jump, Square/X or R2 fire, Circle/B special, Triangle/Y melee (the layout Broforce players report as its pad default), L1/R1 roll, L2 hold to aim (d-pad up tilts the gun up), right stick aims in any direction ahead or up (and turns you to face that side), Options/Start pause, hold the touchpad or Create/Select for the map. Press a button once after the page loads so the browser sees the pad. Explosions rumble pads that support it (Chrome; Firefox has no rumble).

### Aiming, melee bros, random bros and longer maps

- **Aiming:** hold the aim button and the bro keeps facing one way while you walk either way (the legs play the stride backwards). Up tilts the gun; a dotted sight shows where it points. Every bro's main weapon follows the aim: bullets, spreads, lobs (a lob never goes backwards), beams, arcs, the welder, the spores, the drill and the four pickup guns. Shots that steer themselves stay as they were (Johnson's shells, Hypatia's disc, Lamarr's torpedo, Wegener's ground wave). Specials and the mech do not aim.
- **Melee bros:** Mary Anbro (fossil hammer: slow, heavy swings that crush quacks and smash a brick in two hits; special: a meteor slams down a few tiles ahead), Andreas Vesalbro (scalpels: quick slashes; special: a dash 7 tiles ahead that cuts every quack in the way) and Robert Hookbro (a boxing glove on a spring that reaches 3 tiles and throws quacks far; special: one huge spring punch). Fire swings at what is in reach, also up when you aim. A swing knocks riot shields away, cuts enemy shots out of the air, bats grenades and rockets back, and chips blocks, but never the floor you stand on.
- **Random bros:** a new campaign starts with 10 random bros unlocked; free the rest from cages in the missions. The briefing opens on a random one of yours (left/right still picks), and after a death the next bro is any of your unlocked bros at random, except the one who just died.
- **Longer maps:** every campaign mission and the Moon Base are 140 columns longer (376 instead of 236). The extra stretch sits between the main path and the boss arena: a few plain pieces (yard, trenches, hall, fort, acid pit, cliff) plus one piece of the mission's own kind, quacks every 7 columns, an ammo crate and two more checkpoint flags. A mission's difficulty target grows with its length, so the stretch is not empty on early missions. Lab Siege keeps its yard. The full map (Tab) shows the level in two strips.
- **More map variety:** 12 new level pieces (freight train, skyscraper scaffold with a crane-hook lift, jungle canopy, ship deck, castle gatehouse with a portcullis to shoot down, mine with a rotten trestle, ice cavern, magma works, sewer junction, radar base, market street with bounce awnings, water tower you can topple) and 4 new boss arenas (collapsing span, helipad, quarry, factory floor with belts). The stretch now fills its later slots with new pieces that fit the mission's theme. Several pieces keep a steel or brick walkway so the way on survives, and some put barrels in clusters beside the quacks. `NEW_PIECES` (width range and themes) and `NEW_ARENAS` (supported boss types) describe them.

### Safe start and choices

- **Safe start:** every mission begins with 10 quiet seconds (a countdown shows). Quacks do not notice you or shoot; if you shoot one first, that one fights back. No enemy spawns in the first 20 tiles of any map, so the start area stays clear after the 10 seconds too.
- **Briefing choices:** pick which freed bro to deploy (left/right) and one piece of starting gear (down, then left/right): shield, jetpack, overclock, or a shotgun, minigun, rocket launcher or railgun.
- **Fair ranges:** no enemy starts attacking from off the screen (snipers and mortars keep their long reach, but only on screen). Grunts, shields, heavies, medics and gurus notice you at about 12 tiles and hurt you from about 11; half the bros reach 15 tiles or more. The shortest weapons reach further than before: Faraday's welder and Fleming's spores 6 tiles, Hypatia's disc 9, Galileo's lens and Archimedes' rubble 11, Tesla's arc and Meitner's fission 12. Quack bullets fly about 18 tiles instead of 25.
- **What to hit on shielded bosses** (the Succussion Engine, the Zeppelin, the Director and the Pitchman): a second banner explains the order, bouncing arrows marked SHOOT sit over each part to break (or at the screen edge, pointing the way, when it is off the screen), then a red arrow marks the core, gas bag or camera; the boss bar counts the parts left; shooting the shield says what to shoot instead.
- **Every weapon works on every boss.** Beams, arcs, the drill, the welder, spores and the ground wave hit boss parts (the Engine's vats, the Zeppelin's gondolas, the Director's lights) just like bullets. The Zeppelin sinks low for a troop drop every 7 s, so its gondolas and gas bag come into range; the Director and the Pitchman swoop down every few seconds for a close-up once their lights are gone. A falling block that hits a boss shatters for 8 damage instead of killing it outright. A boss only calms down if you are well outside its arena and more than 10 tiles away, and any hit keeps it awake.
- **The Crystal Crawler and the Quartz Queen:** shots on the crystal body chip it for 35% damage (head shots still do full damage), a blinking red mark shows where it will burst out of the sand, and a leap ends with its head stuck in the sand for a second, taking 50% more damage.
- **Losing a bro:** when you die, every quack forgets you, enemy shots still in the air fizzle out, and the next bro gets 3 quiet seconds (unless he shoots first). A boss calms down soon after you die, or after you stay out of its arena for 2.5 s; its homing missiles fizzle. It keeps its damage and wakes again when you walk back in. Quacks that lose sight of you give up after 2 s.
- **Mission select:** on the world map, left/right picks any mission you have reached before in this browser.

### Story

The war is told in four acts: **Act I, The Placebo Effect** (missions 1-4), **Act II, The Broadcast** (5-10), **Act III, Road to the Citadel** (11-17) and the **Finale, The Big Orange** (18). The cast talks over the radio during missions, with portraits: Prof. Peer Review runs HQ; Dr. Placebo is the League's general; Professor Quackenbush, whose paper Peer Review rejected in 98 (it said water has feelings), is the League's voice on the radio; King Citrus is its face. Each mission has lines at the start, at the first checkpoint, when the boss wakes and when it goes down; freed bros say something when you let them out, HQ sometimes comments when you lose a bro, and special events get a line too. Each briefing names the act and shows a League message HQ intercepted. Placebo is caught in Act I and escapes in Act III; at the Citadel, King Citrus turns out to be a robot with Quackenbush inside. The end screen rolls an epilogue for the cast, then shows the run's stats.

### Special events

From mission 3, a campaign mission can have one special event (more likely later on; each mission's roll is fixed). Events never start during the quiet start, a boss fight, a respawn or extraction, or in the bonus modes.
- **Oppenbro drops in:** Robert Oppenbro parachutes in, walks to a League target ahead, plants his nuke, shouts GET CLEAR and runs. Stay outside the red ring.
- **League airstrike:** a siren and a red strip with a sweeping warning line, then bombers carpet-bomb it.
- **Supply drop:** a gun crate and an ammo crate on parachutes, sometimes an extra bro.
- **Ambush:** 4 to 7 paratroopers land around you.
- **Golden quack:** a gold quack runs off; kill it within 16 s for an extra bro.
- **Meteor shower** (Flat Earth, observatory and Moon levels): rocks with a red mark a second before impact.
- **Blackout** (dark levels): 14 s with only lamps and muzzle flashes for light.

### Campaign

Title menu (Campaign, Lab Siege, Boss Rush, Moon Base, Trophies), then a world map of the 18 stops, then a briefing from HQ (Prof. Peer Review) with the mission's objectives. Each level has required objectives (they must be done before the extraction chopper comes) and bonus ones (free every scientist, find the three intel files; each bonus is worth 2000 points). Intel files tell the story; the debrief after each level moves it on and the ending wraps it up. Lives and freed bros carry over between missions. Checkpoint flags refill your specials, and the next bro parachutes in over the last flag you raised.

- **Mission 1, Placebo Island:** smash 3 placebo vats, destroy the Homeopathitron.
- **Mission 2, Detox Desert:** topple the radio mast, shoot down the Detoxkopter, kill the Crystal Crawler.
- **Mission 3, Cryo Summit:** wipe 2 server racks, stop the Snake Oil Tank (Dr. Placebo escapes in a pod).
- **Story:** the Anti-Science League wants to take over the world. Dr. Placebo is its general for the first four missions; its leader is King Citrus, the Big Bad Orange, a fictional giant angry orange who preaches vitamin C megadoses.
- **Mission 4, Dilution Plant:** smash 2 dilution vats, shut down the **Succussion Engine**: a hammer drops where you stand (a red column warns you for about a second; it follows you slowly), it sprays diluted solution now and then and sends out a quack. Its core sits up on the gantry, shielded until you break its three stabiliser vats, which stand on the floor; then the core drops on its chain to shooting height, a second hammer joins, and rubble falls from the roof. The hammer's shockwave runs along the floor: jump it.

- **Mission 5, Flat Earth Station:** jam 3 propaganda antennas, climb (or blast through) the ice wall they call the edge of the world, and bring down the **Flat Earth Zeppelin**: shoot its four gondolas off from the tower tops, then the gas bag. It bombs you and drops paratroopers.
- **Mission 6, Chemtrail Airfield:** topple 3 spray towers and shoot down the **Chemtrail Copter** (a tougher Detoxkopter) over the tower arena.
- **Mission 7, Quartz Caverns:** a dark cave lit by glowing crystals. Smash 3 crystal-charging vats and kill the **Quartz Queen** (a tougher Crystal Crawler) in the sand pit.
- **Mission 8, Miasma Marsh:** rain, bog pits and bridges. Wipe 2 research servers and stop the **Miasma Machine** (a tougher Snake Oil Tank).
- **Mission 9, Zodiac Observatory:** snow on a night summit. Knock down 3 horoscope dishes and scrap the **Zodiac Mech** (a tougher Homeopathitron).
- **Mission 10, Perpetual Motion Works:** a rusty factory with chimneys (climb them, or shoot out their crate base and drop them). Smash 3 demo engines and stop **the Pitchman**, a crane camera whose stage lights shield it.
- Missions 6 to 10 are built from a set of level pieces (camp, yard, hall, bridge over a pit, cliff with a tunnel, fort, chimneys) and four boss arenas.
- **Mission 11, Crop Circle Fields:** daytime farmland. Topple 3 fake alien beacons and pop the **UFO Hoax Balloon** (a Zeppelin-type boss).
- **Mission 12, Pyramid Power Plant:** desert. Smash 3 pyramid batteries and kill the **Sand Scarab** (a crawler).
- **Mission 13, Homeopathic Hot Springs:** snow. Wipe 2 booking servers and scrap the **Steam Tank**.
- **Mission 14, Ley Line Lighthouse:** night. Knock down 3 ley-line antennas and shoot down the **Astral Kopter**; its pilot opens the way to the Citadel.
- **Difficulty curve:** each mission is set to a threat level that climbs from the first to the finale (14 on mission 1, 54 on the Citadel; a grunt counts 1, a heavy or sniper 3). Crowded early maps lose quacks, sparse later ones get reinforcements next to quacks already there (never in the safe start). Heavy hitters arrive step by step: grenadiers, bombers and medics from mission 3; flamers, turrets, rocketeers and gurus from 4; jetpacks, camera drones and heavies from 5; snipers and brutes from 6; mortars and preachers from 7. Early on, quacks react later and shoot slower and bosses have less health (65% on mission 1). The same mission always has the same quacks. Bonus modes play at full strength.
- **Missions 6 to 14 rebuilt for variety:** each stronghold is now built from its own set of pieces (about 40 in all: runway, crashed plane, hangar, tunnels, mine-cart chasm, shaft, boardwalks, observatory dome, foundry, crane yard, silos, pyramid, hot pools, spa hotel, lighthouse, rooftops, cistern, pipeline and more), so no two share a layout, and each boss fights in its own arena (airstrip, cavern, bridge, dome, crane, vents, headland, rooftop, plus the older flat, towers, sand and stage). Every theme draws its own skyline: pyramids, sea and lighthouse, farm, crystals, swamp, observatory, gears, cooling towers, spa, pipes, neon, orchard.
- **Level mechanics:** conveyor belts that carry whoever stands on them, wind gusts that push you and your shots, steam vents that launch you upward, lifts, a rising acid flood, anti-gravity fields, cracked floors that give way (with a ledge below), and stalactites in the caves. Belts in the Perpetual Motion Works, the Water Works and the Juice Pipeline; wind at the Chemtrail Airfield, the Pyramid Power Plant, the Ley Line Lighthouse and the Neon Wellness District; vents in the Miasma Marsh, the factories, the Hot Springs and the Water Works; anti-gravity at the Zodiac Observatory; the flood in the Water Works.
- **Mission 15, Structured Water Works:** a sewer. Smash the vortex tanks while the acid rises, kill the **Sludge Serpent**.
- **Mission 16, Neon Wellness District:** a night city of rooftops and gondolas. Topple the billboard masts and cancel **The Influencer**.
- **Mission 17, Juice Pipeline:** an orange grove crossed by belts and steam valves. Wipe the shipping servers and scrap **The Zestinator**.
- **Mission 18, Citrus Citadel:** pour out 3 megadose vats in the juice works, fight through the rally and the citadel walls, then juice **King Citrus**. He hops with shockwaves, rolls across the throne room (and is dizzy, taking double damage, after hitting a wall), spits seeds, squirts juice puddles, calls in quacks, and when furious rains vitamin pills.

### Modes and trophies

- **Lab Siege:** hold a walled yard against endless waves. A boss every fifth wave, supplies between waves, an extra bro every third wave. Every bro is unlocked. Your best wave is saved.
- **Boss Rush:** all 19 campaign bosses back to back on 5 lives, against the clock. Your best time is saved.
- **Moon Base:** a bonus mission in low gravity (jumps go much higher). Jam 3 radio masts on a fake film set, cross the crater field and take down **the Director**, a crane camera with a spotlight that locks on and fires, sandbag drops and paratrooper extras.
- **Trophies:** 19 of them, for things like a x10 combo, 500 blocks broken, a flawless mission, a mission under 3 minutes, Lab Siege waves 5 and 15, the Boss Rush, the Moon, and 10 camera drones downed. Career counters and trophies are saved in this browser. A toast pops up when you earn one.

### Maps

- **World map:** islands for Act I, two continents for Acts II and III and the Citadel on its own island, with coastlines, beaches and shallow water. Each stop has a landmark (palms, a glowing pyramid, a lighthouse with a sweeping beam, crop fields with the UFO balloon, a crystal cave, a city with neon, an orange grove...). The sea moves, clouds drift, and day slowly turns to night. Freed stops fly a science flag, the next target a League banner, and stops you have not reached lie under fog; the Citadel looms as a giant orange fortress under a storm cloud. The plane flies along the route to the stop you pick. Act labels sit over each region, a bar counts freed missions, and a card shows the selected stop.

A minimap in the bottom-right corner shows the level around you. Hold Tab for the whole level with markers for you, quacks, cages, targets, intel and the boss, plus the objective list.

### Levels, enemies and bosses

- **Level 1, Placebo Island:** beach, bunker, acid bridge, cliff with a watchtower, cliff fort. Boss: the **Homeopathitron**, a walking pill mech. It fires spreads, jumps for shockwaves and summons quacks. Below 40% health it enters overdose mode: faster, with homing missiles.
- **Level 2, Detox Desert:** dunes, a two-floor Wellness Spa, an oil field and an acid canyon. Mid-boss: the **Detoxkopter**, which strafes, drops bombs and hovers low to fire missiles; climb the brick towers to hit it. Final boss: the **Crystal Crawler**, a worm that tunnels through the sand, bursts out in arcs or rears up spitting acid. Only its head takes bullets.
- **Level 3, Cryo Summit:** snowy slopes with a frozen pond, the Cryo Spa (a mech is parked outside), a chasm of freezing water under a brittle ice bridge, a tunnel with icicles that drop on whatever walks beneath them, a snow fort. Final boss: the **Snake Oil Tank**, driven by Dr. Placebo. It keeps its distance lobbing shells from a turret that tracks you, rolls in with a flamethrower of burning snake oil, and below half health it mortars and rams.
- Ice blocks are slippery and shatter easily.
- Lives and freed bros carry over between levels. Game over restarts the current level.
- **Quacks:** grunt, riot shield (blocks shots from the front; punch the shield away), heavy (20 hp, spins up a minigun), scout (runs off whistling and wakes every quack nearby), grenadier (punch grenades back), rocketeer (kneels and aims; shoot the rocket down), guru (calls in paratroopers), paratrooper, suicide bomber, attack dog (leaps at you), plus turrets.
- **Bros (12):** Einstein, Curie, Tesla, Newton and Franklin on mission 1. Mission 2 adds Ada Lovebro (three-card spread; drone) and Charles Darbro (homing finches; natural selection). Mission 3 adds Galileo Galilbro (a sun lens that sets things alight; a sun that orbits you) and Alan Turbro (ricochet rounds; hacks the quacks on screen into fighting each other). Mission 4 adds Katherine Johnbro (shells that land on the nearest quack; orbital strikes), Lise Meitbro (fission shots that split twice; a chain reaction that marches ahead) and Archimedes Bro (a screw drill that bores through rock; a lever that throws everything on screen into the air).
- **Newest quacks:** the **preacher** holds up a book and preaches; every quack near him reloads faster and takes 40% less damage for 6 s (they get a halo), so shoot him first. The **mortar crew** marks the ground where you stand with a blinking red cross, then lobs a shell onto it; keep moving, or get close and he backs off. The **camera drone** patrols in the air; when it has you in frame it shows REC, then dives at you and blows up. Shoot it down over quacks to hit them with its blast.
- **More quacks:** jetpack troopers hover and shoot down; snipers track you with a laser sight that locks before one fast shot; flamethrower troopers burn what is in front of them and their tanks blow when they die; brutes charge through walls and other quacks and are dazed when they hit one; medics revive fresh quack corpses (three times at most), so shoot them first.
- **Objects and items:** ladders, bounce pads, proximity mines, alarm boxes (once a quack nearby spots you, it calls paratroopers; shoot it first), fuel tanks (huge blasts), blue item crates (shield that stops one killing blow, overclock for double fire rate, jetpack, extra bro), intel files, and objective targets.
- Supply crates refill one special. Animations: running leans forward and kicks up dust, turning at speed skids, the gun swings with the stride when you're not shooting, hit quacks flinch, freed bros flip out of the cage. Steel piles under the acid bridges stay up even if the crates burn away.

### Weapons

- **Pickup guns** come from orange crates (and sometimes from blue item crates). Each replaces your bro's weapon until its ammo runs out: shotgun (7 pellets, big kick), rocket launcher, minigun, and railgun (one line through every quack and every block that is not steel). Ammo shows on the HUD.
- **Upgrades:** Einstein's photons pass through the first quack, and he fires faster during time dilation; Curie's vials leave a radiation puddle; Tesla's arc chains to four quacks and stuns them; Newton's apples bounce once before going off; Franklin's X-ray cuts through one block; Ada fires five cards and her drone lasts 12 s; Darwin sends three finches.
- **Bros 19 to 22:** Carl Sagbro (slow glowing orbs that pass through everything; star rain), Alfred Wegbro (a ripple along the ground that throws quacks up; the ground splits under every quack on screen), Edwin Hubbro (shots that hit harder the further they fly; everything on screen flung away), Dmitri Mendelbro (each shot a random element: sodium blast, floating helium, piercing iron, fast neon, uranium puddle; eight element blasts).
- **New bros (18 in all):** Gregor Mendbro (pea shooter; three pea turrets), Louis Pastbro (darts that poison; a heat wave that cooks nearby quacks), Michael Farabro (an arc welder that cuts quacks and stone; a cage that bounces every shot back for 6 s), Hypatia Bro (a disc that flies out and comes back through quacks; six discs that spiral out), Richard Feynbro (wavy shots that pass through two quacks; shots in every direction), Alexander Flembro (spores that make quacks sick, and sickness spreads; every quack on screen falls sick).
- **Bro 35, Robert Oppenbro:** a neutron pistol whose bolts leave a short radiation glow, and one special per life: **Trinity**, a nuke. He lobs it about 7 tiles ahead; it counts down 3 s with a red danger ring (5 tiles). Then a white flash, a fireball, shockwaves, a mushroom cloud that lingers, falling ash and a crater about 13 tiles wide that you can climb out of (it never touches steel, the bottom rows or the extraction pad). Every ordinary enemy nearby dies; bosses take 25% of their health over 10 hits. Inside the ring you die; outside, you are thrown clear.
- **Bros 29 to 34:** Alfred Nobro (sticky dynamite that clings to walls and quacks; a fan of five charges), Niels Brohr (shots that hop through up to two blocks and ignore riot shields; a quantum jump up to 8 tiles ahead with a burst at both ends), Hedy Lamarbro (a radio torpedo that homes in on the height of the quack ahead; frequency hopping fizzles enemy shots and jams their guns for 6 s), Antoine Lavoisbro (an oxygen torch that sets quacks alight; combustion sets every quack on screen on fire), Jane Goodbro (a pant-hoot that rolls out in widening rings, stunning each quack at most once a second; a troop of three chimps) and Mae Jemisbro (tap for a bolt, hold to charge a plasma ball that goes through three quacks; zero-g makes quacks on screen float, then crash).
- **Gun feel:** every gun has its own muzzle flash shape and colour, recoil that kicks the arm and muzzle, a barrel that glows and smokes after hard firing, casings from bullet guns and vent sparks from energy guns, a glow while slow guns get ready, and small idle animations (flicker, sparks, steam, a fizzing fuse, a pilot flame). The minigun spins up, the shotgun pumps out a hull, the rocket launcher has a back-blast, the railgun coil recharges. Fast shots leave tracers; impacts throw sparks on steel, dust on earth, chips on brick, splinters on crates and shards on ice, and leave holes or scorch marks. Explosions add a shockwave ring, spark streaks, a smoke column, embers and a burnt strip on the ground; killing a heavy, a brute or a boss freezes the frame for a moment.
- **Bros 23 to 28:** Stephen Hawkbro (a spray of tiny particles; a black hole that pulls quacks in, eats blocks and goes off), Grace Hopbro (three-round bursts; marks every quack on screen as a bug that takes double damage for 8 s), Chien-Shiung Wubro (a shot forward and its mirror image backward; flips every quack on screen round and knocks it flat), Dorothy Hodgbro (a fan of crystal shards; crystal spikes burst from the ground ahead), Leonardo da Vinbro (crossbow bolts that pass through two quacks; a flying machine for 8 s) and Ernest Rutherbro (alpha particles that bounce off walls; a fan of twelve).
- **Who is in a cage** is decided when you open it, not before (a mystery bro flickers inside). While any bros are still locked you always get one of them, each equally likely. Once all are free, a shuffled bag hands out every bro before any repeats, and never the bro you are playing.
- **Hit boxes:** bullets are tested along the path they travelled each frame, so fast shots cannot skip through anything; enemy shots must touch a box a pixel inside your sprite. The pause menu can show all hitboxes.

### Moves

- **Dash-roll:** a quick burst along the ground (a roll) or through the air; bullets and dog bites pass through you. 0.6 s cooldown, shown under your portrait.
- **Wall jump:** jump while touching a wall without holding into it and you kick off the other way. Holding into the wall still climbs.
- **Ground pound:** Down + punch in mid-air slams straight down. It breaks the floor, keeps drilling through up to 4 layers of earth, crates or bricks, and throws nearby quacks.

### Bro-Mech

Walk into a parked mech (Level 1 cliff fort, Level 2 canyon, Level 3 outside the spa) to climb in. It bulldozes soft walls just by walking into them, fires a heavy gun (fire) and rockets (special), smashes through blocks with its fist (punch), shakes the ground when it lands, and takes 24 hits for you. Dash jumps out backwards; if it is destroyed it blows up and throws you clear.

### Destruction

- **Only loose blocks fall.** Earth and steel stay where they are, even with nothing under them. Brick, crates and ice are loose: they stay up only while a chain of loose blocks connects them to earth underneath or to steel on any side. Loose blocks have a dashed yellow outline, and hazard stripes mark where they rest on earth: cut there and that whole set comes down. Blocks about to fall shake and their outline turns red.

- Blasts throw big lumps of the blocks they break; a fast lump knocks out the quack it hits.
- Blasts punch holes in back walls, so the sky shows through the bunker and the spa.
- Fire on a crate burns it until it gives and creeps along neighbouring crates, so bridges burn down piece by piece.
- Red airstrike crates drop a pickup that calls in a jet. It carpet-bombs the ground ahead of you.

### Graphics

- **A living world:** plants grow on every level (grass tufts, ferns, flowers, round bushes, reeds, glowing mushrooms in the caves, weeds in the factories, shrubs in the desert and snow) and vines hang under ledges. They bend when anyone walks through them, sway in a light wind, are cut by shots and blasts, burn in fire, and grow back after 15 to 30 s, starting as small sprouts. Critters live there too: birds, crabs, lizards, frogs, butterflies, dragonflies, bats, rats, hares and tumbleweeds, by level. They scatter when you come close or at gunfire and blasts; birds fly off and new ones land later; shots and blasts kill them. Blasted earth loses its grass and grows it back over about 14 s. The Moon stays lifeless.
- Textured earth with layers, pebbles and roots; grass with a ragged edge that hangs over drops, swaying blades and the odd flower; each brick shaded on its own, with moss, sand or snow on top of walls.
- Two layers of drifting clouds and a second, nearer hill layer with a lit ridge; soft shadows on the ground under every character; fireflies on the island, in the swamp and in the caves, dust in the desert and factories, embers at the Citadel.
- **Blood and gore** (pause menu: off, on, extra): shots spray blood onto the wall behind the target, corpses leave pools, and on extra explosions leave a red mist and meat chunks that drip as they fly. Off turns blood into grey dust and explosions into puffs.
- Dynamic lighting: the night is dark, and lamps, muzzle flashes, explosions, fire, acid, rockets, crystals and pickups light it up with coloured glow. A vignette darkens the edges.
- 1px outlines on all characters; tiles shade their exposed edges and get darker the deeper they sit.
- Explosions are lumpy fireballs that cool from white to red and rise. Rain and lightning on the island, blowing sand in the desert.
- Snow, aurora and a pine skyline on the Cryo Summit.
- Title screen plays over the level with explosions going off in the background.
- Pause menu (P): screen shake, scanlines, music, lighting, minimap, hitboxes, blood and gore, ambience (all saved in this browser), restart level, quit to title.
- New HUD: portrait, specials as flasks, the bro reserve as heads, dash cooldown, score. Kills inside 1.6 s chain into a combo that multiplies the points; score, run total and best combo show on the end screens, and the best run is saved in this browser and shown on the title.

### Animation and sound

- **Sound:** every effect runs through one mixer with a compressor (louder without crackling) and a short echo on explosions and roars. Sounds are panned left or right to where they happen and get quieter far away; each play shifts the pitch a little. Gunshots, explosions, punches, hits, crumbling blocks, rockets, the machine gun, jumps and deaths are layered with a low thump. New: footsteps when running, and level ambience (rain as single drops and soft plops over a low bed on the island and in the marsh, wind in the desert, snow and sky levels, a hum in the factories, cave air with drips), plus birds chirping and frogs croaking now and then.

- Characters are drawn in layers: a 6-frame run cycle (far leg darker), an upper body that bobs with the stride and breathes when idle, and a front arm with its own poses (aim, punch, climb, throw, both arms waving when on fire). Jump, fall, kneel and wall-climb poses.
- Each quack type has its own head, body, weapon and tells: the heavy's barrels spin, the rocketeer's sight line blinks, the guru's orb glows while chanting, the scout's whistle sends out rings.
- The mech walks on two jointed legs with footfalls that shake the screen. The kopter tilts into its motion. The crawler's body follows its head like a chain.
- A small step sequencer plays bass, drums and a lead line, with a faster track during boss fights. M mutes everything.

### Physics and details

- Structures stand only while a chain of blocks links them to bedrock or a steel girder. Cut one loose and it shakes, then falls block by block, crushing whatever is under it. The bunker roof, the watchtower and the acid bridge can all be dropped.
- Shot quacks fly back as ragdolls that bounce, settle and can be shot again. Explosions and falling blocks tear them into gibs. Blood and scorch marks stick to the blocks they hit.
- Broken blocks throw chunks that bounce and settle. The photon rifle ejects shell casings. Every gun has a muzzle flash.
- Blasts push the player, bodies, barrels and lamps, and leave small fires that set quacks running in a panic and burn through wooden crates. Propane tanks fly like rockets when shot or punched.
- Punch launches quacks, corpses, barrels and tanks. A punched bomber flies off and goes off where it lands.
- Jump buffering, landing dust and squash, footstep dust, a short freeze on big hits, quacks that alert each other.
- Back walls behind broken earth and inside the bunker, swinging lamps, a cloth flag, palms, signs, sandbags, searchlights, and a rope ladder on the chopper.
